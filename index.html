Germany Live Power Grid & Carbon Dashboard

A single-screen, real-time view of the German power system: live generation mix, a computed grid carbon intensity, day-ahead prices, and live green-power for all 16 federal states. Built from public open-data feeds with no API keys and no database. A power-operations command centre in Streamlit and Plotly.

Python Streamlit Plotly Climate Open Data
How it is built

What it shows, and why it is useful

At a glance: how much of Germany’s electricity is renewable right now, how clean it is, what it costs, and how the 16 states compare. The practical use is timing. A short forecast shows when power will be greenest and cheapest in a given state, so you can pick the best window to charge an electric car or run heavy appliances. For anyone following the energy transition, it is a live, honest read on how the grid is tracking toward the 2030 renewable target, and a clear example of turning open data into something people can act on.

How it is built

  1. Live, key-free feeds. Energy-Charts for the national mix and day-ahead price, Corrently GrünstromIndex for per-postcode green power, Open-Meteo for solar and wind.
  2. Carbon computed, not fetched. No CO₂ endpoint exists, so grid carbon intensity is derived from the live mix using IPCC lifecycle emission factors.
  3. All 16 federal states. The green-power feed is keyed by postcode, so each state is sampled at its capital for even national coverage.
  4. Live solar field. One batched Open-Meteo call returns a ~300-point irradiance grid, rendered as a heat layer behind the state bubbles.
  5. Command-centre layout. A single-screen, Z-pattern design: headline KPIs across the top, the map as the anchor, the actionable “when is power greenest” forecast bottom-right.
  6. Simple to run. No database to manage; pushed to GitHub and served on Streamlit Community Cloud from a single file.

A/B Testing Framework

A full experiment lifecycle tool, from "should I even test this?" through statistical analysis and plain-English recommendations. Covers idea validation, sample-size calculation, frequentist and Bayesian analysis, and an LLM verdict layer. Most A/B testing tools start after data is collected. This one starts before.

Python Streamlit Statistics Bayesian LLM
How it is built

What it does

Four steps, from the idea to the ship decision.

  1. Should I test this? Routes an idea to the right method (A/B test, user research, feature flag, or just ship) and generates a structured brief. LLM-powered but grounded in a decision rubric, not a free-form chat.
  2. How many users do I need? Power analysis that forces a commitment to baseline rate, minimum detectable effect, significance and power before any traffic is spent. Sensitivity and power curves show the cost of each assumption.
  3. What do the results mean? A sample ratio mismatch check, a two-proportion z-test and a Beta-Binomial Bayesian analysis, then a plain-English ship or hold recommendation.
  4. Workspace. Brief and results-doc generators, Markdown export for Notion, Confluence or Linear, and a searchable experiment registry that persists to Supabase when configured.

How it is built

  • MDE as the business gate. Statistical significance alone does not trigger a ship recommendation; the result has to clear the minimum detectable effect committed to up front.
  • Frequentist and Bayesian side by side. A p-value for rigour, P(B > A) for intuition.
  • Opinionated flow. You cannot run the results interpreter before committing to an MDE.
  • Local LLM. Runs on Ollama, so no API key and it works offline.
  • Importable stats. The stats modules are plain Python, usable as a library outside the app.

Tamil Nadu Temple Metro தமிழ்நாடு கோயில் மெட்ரோ

Thirty two great temples of Tamil Nadu drawn as a subway network. The lines follow real pilgrimage routes: the Pancha Bhoota shrines of the five elements, the six abodes of Murugan, the nine planetary temples around Kumbakonam, the great living Chola temples. Interchanges fall where those pilgrimages actually cross. Tap a station and the temple's kopuram rises with its story, Tamil first, English beneath.

Tamil temples hold a great deal of the region's history in their stone: Pallava and Chola dynasties, Bhakti poetry, astronomy set into the Navagraha shrines, whole towns that grew up around a single gopuram. They remain central to Tamil culture, and I wanted that history somewhere people could wander through rather than only read about.

Why most A/B tests fail before the data is collected.
